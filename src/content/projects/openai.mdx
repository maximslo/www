## The problem

### 1. AI chatbots are becoming the front door to the news.

Readers increasingly ask ChatGPT or Claude instead of visiting The Post. Reddit and TikTok compete for attention — LLMs compete for the relationship itself. Every question answered inside a chatbot absorbs The Post's reporting without attribution, without a visit, without a subscription. The Post risks becoming an invisible backend source: read by models, uncredited, unvisited.

This is "platformization" reaching its endpoint: news used to travel from the source to the reader. Then it traveled through intermediaries — feeds, platforms, creators. The newest intermediary is the AI chatbot, and it's the first one that replaces the destination entirely.

### 2. Chatbots are fast. Journalism is trusted. Readers shouldn't have to pick one.

Everything a chatbot is good at — summarizing, searching, speed — is exactly where traditional news feels slow. Everything a chatbot is bad at — accuracy, sourcing, accountability — is exactly what journalism is built on.

That tradeoff was the design problem: bring the speed and synthesis readers are already choosing to The Post, without ever letting an answer drift from verified reporting.

## Opportunity


### With its own LLM interface, The Post can reclaim its relationship with readers.

Right now, other platforms own the interface layer — and they're monetizing The Post's journalism without crediting it.

But journalism is the most socially valuable, verified, hard-won information in the world, and that's The Post's moat. LLMs are becoming commoditized, and they have knowledge cutoffs; fresh, accountable reporting is the one thing they can't generate. The Post doesn't need to out-model the AI labs. It needs to become the interface to what only it has.

<img src="/projects/twiz3.png" width="1314" height="906" alt="GenAI opportunity" class="outlined" />

## The solution

### Ask The Post: the speed of a chatbot, grounded entirely in Post journalism.

Summarize, search, and ask — the interactions readers are leaving for ChatGPT — powered exclusively by The Post's reporting, with every claim cited back to the original article. Retrieval is scoped to The Post's corpus, so answers can't drift into hallucination, and citations are always real journalism.

And because the platform is agentic, it goes one step further: what if the news didn't just inform you, but became a trigger for action in the world?

## Market Research

### Readers don't want AI instead of journalism.

They want AI to make it more accessible. Like a museum guide or sommelier.

According to the 2026 Reuters Institute Digital News Report, most people think AI will make the news less transparent, accurate, and trustworthy. Yet reader behavior is already moving toward chatbots — they're choosing speed and synthesis even though trust hasn't followed.

That gap is the tradeoff I designed against: readers shouldn't have to trade the accuracy of journalism for the convenience of a chatbot. AI should serve the reader's relationship with journalism, not replace it.

## Engineering

### An LLM's knowledge is frozen at training time. News is, by definition, what happened after.

The knowledge cutoff isn't a problem to patch — it's the reason The Post wins. The core architectural decision was to never treat the model as the source of facts. The model reasons and writes; the knowledge lives in a retrieval index of Post reporting that updates the moment a story publishes. Freshness is solved by construction, not by retraining.

That decision shaped everything downstream — shipped to production serving 3M+ readers across The Post's site and apps, with engagement growing 3×:

<div class="cols">
  <div>
    <h4>Fresh by construction</h4>
    <p>Every answer is generated from reporting retrieved at query time. New articles are indexed as they publish, so there is no cutoff to hit.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h4>Multi-turn grounding</h4>
    <p>Naive RAG breaks on follow-ups — "what happened next?" retrieves nothing. Rewriting queries with full conversational context took follow-up accuracy to 100%.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h4>Speed parity</h4>
    <p>Retrieval adds hops, and the whole thesis fails if it's slower than ChatGPT. Optimizing retrieval, inference, and context handling cut end-to-end latency by up to 4×.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="cols centered">
  <div>
    <h4>Grounded citations</h4>
    <p>Retrieval is scoped exclusively to The Post's corpus, so every claim maps to a real article. A hallucinated source is impossible by design.</p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <h4>Model-agnostic core</h4>
    <p>If LLMs are commoditized, the platform should swap them freely. The moat is the corpus and the retrieval layer — infrastructure (including the first production Table-RAG and MCP servers) now used by 2,000+ employees.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## What I learned

### Wear the users shoes.

At first, I tried solving AI news selfishly.

The Washington Post fell behind during the print -> digital shift so for digital -> agentic, executives panicked. Throwing anything at the wall to see if it stuck, teams led to build a chatbot and AI-generated podcast.

AI is not a solution to the suffocation of high-quality journalism done a billionare owner.

But the real juice is in how this is beneficial to actual readers. Wins will come from that.
